# Spoken Vietnamese Evaluation Reference

Use this after a draft exists.

A strong paragraph can be paraphrased as one ordinary sentence explaining what changed and why it leads forward. Sentences use concrete subjects and verbs; terminology is introduced only when necessary and explained immediately.

Listen for:

- causal relations that remain clear when spoken;
- contrast between explanatory sentences and concise discoveries/consequences;
- qualifications that protect accuracy without taking over the paragraph;
- facts ordered by listener need rather than source, claim ID or chronology;
- semantic repetition disguised as new wording;
- emotional weight produced by historical situation rather than hyperbole.

These are outcome cues, not a compulsory cadence or paragraph formula. A writer may solve the same listening problem through different valid structures.
