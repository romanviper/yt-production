# Outcome Evaluation Standard

Evaluate the draft that exists, not whether it followed one preferred storytelling method.

Principle:

> **outcome → diagnosis**

not:

> method compliance → outcome

## Primary outcome judgment

Judge first:

- **Section objective:** does the section reach its assigned exit state and advance the whole-product progression?
- **Listener progression:** is the listener pulled through a meaningful change in curiosity, tension, surprise, understanding or emotional weight when appropriate?
- **Authorship:** does the narration feel intentionally shaped, or like research information being recited?
- **Experience formation:** does prose transform information into an experience, world, process, relationship or conceptual model appropriate to the material?
- **Listening quality:** is the narration crafted for spoken delivery and coherent without depending on prose-like visual parsing?
- **Causal clarity:** are mechanisms and consequences understandable without false certainty?
- **Continuity:** does the section inherit and hand off state correctly?
- **Evidence integrity:** no invention, unsupported function/causation, truth-ceiling expansion or suppressed qualification/contradiction.

A draft can pass through many different narrative routes. Do not fail it because it lacks a carrier, before/after, raw clue, process sequence or recount-before-interpret structure.

## Diagnose only after an outcome fails

Once a material outcome problem is observed, use mechanics as optional diagnostic heuristics.

Possible questions:

- Would a concrete object/person/process make the listener's mental model clearer?
- Is interpretation arriving before enough factual grounding?
- Would contrast or before/after expose the change more efficiently?
- Is a process sequence available in the evidence but unused?
- Is explanation repeating a conclusion that already landed?
- Is the narrator repeatedly telling the audience what to think rather than creating the needed progression?

These questions suggest repairs; they are not universal requirements.

## Conditional audience-agency integrity

Pseudo-agency is only relevant when narration **claims or strongly stages an audience inference/discovery**.

If the draft says or implies “you can see/notice/realize X,” test whether the audience had access to the relevant raw evidence before specialist classification. If not, flag pseudo-agency.

Do not require every section to let the audience infer from a raw clue. Straight expert narration is valid when it is the better form.

## Common failure patterns

- **Document mode:** information is accurate but arranged mainly as memo/lecture rather than an authored listening progression.
- **Explanatory echo:** the same idea is shown, interpreted, paraphrased and summarized without a new fact, boundary or consequence.
- **Meta-narration overload:** narration repeatedly describes how the audience should process the story instead of moving the story/understanding itself.
- **Causal blur:** chronology, correlation, mechanism and causation are collapsed.
- **Evidence overreach:** a new claim/generalization appears outside the approved ceiling or retrieved source detail is promoted into a new thesis without research validation.

Use deletion, concrete-detail, sequence, contrast or scale-shift tests only when they help diagnose one of these observed failures.

## Diagnose the responsible layer

- `prose_execution`: objective/evidence territory are sound; authorship, pacing, structure, wording or listening experience failed.
- `product_architecture`: objective, section boundary, whole-product progression or evidence territory is wrong.
- `evidence`: required factual support is missing, contradicted, insufficient or would require truth-ceiling expansion.

There is no active story-plan/local-design authority in the current path. Route the issue to the layer that actually made the decision: draft, outline or research.

Give an observable acceptance test. Do not create a global writer rule for a one-off failure.
