Status: `PROMISING_BUT_TRUTH_BOUNDARY_FAIL`

This probe is the first result in the current sequence that changes the product type in a meaningful way. It is no longer primarily an essay, evidence tour, or artifact catalogue. The listener can follow a material process with forward motion, ordinary physical action, and a clear change in how quantity becomes inspectable.

That improvement must be preserved.

Do **not** respond to this feedback by redesigning the architecture, changing Historical Substrate, expanding the notebook, adding more style rules, or restoring research-ledger metadata to the Writer context.

The next iteration has one narrow job:

> Preserve the narrative movement and spoken quality of v1 while preventing the Writer from inventing causal bridges or genealogical links that the historical authority does not support.

## What worked and must remain frozen

1. `writer-notebook.md` is the Writer's primary historical workspace rather than Historical Substrate, claim ledger, material ledger, or provenance metadata.
2. The Writer receives a short editorial angle rather than an answer-shaped thesis.
3. The Writer owns composition and may ignore most of the notebook.
4. The Writer creates only a high-level composition decision before drafting.
5. The Writer may use bounded representative ordinary action.
6. Uncertainty and truth boundaries remain mostly silent constraints rather than prose obligations.
7. The Writer context remains free of previous probes, evaluator diagnosis, competitor prose, architecture discussion, and operational/runtime noise.
8. The probe remains a contiguous unfinished slice rather than a miniature complete P01.
9. No repair loop or self-review occurs before human cold read.

These choices produced the first substantial improvement in forward motion and natural spoken narration. Treat them as experiment controls for the next round.

## What failed

The Writer created narrative momentum partly by filling unsupported causal gaps.

### Failure 1 — invented motive / problem-solution bridge

The draft says, in effect:

- loose counters are vulnerable to someone adding or removing them;
- therefore enclosing them in clay naturally appears as a response.

The notebook explicitly does not establish fraud prevention, tamper prevention, distance administration, or another single motive as the cause of envelopes.

The problem is not merely one inaccurate sentence. It creates a causal grammar for the passage:

`loose counters are vulnerable → envelope solves the vulnerability`

That story is more certain than the evidence.

### Failure 2 — unsupported absolute functional claim

The draft states that while an envelope remains intact, the quantity inside cannot be changed.

The authority supports that the enclosed counters are not directly visible while the envelope remains closed and that opening the sealed object destroys that sealed state. It does not authorize the absolute proposition that alteration is impossible under every relevant circumstance.

### Failure 3 — exterior impressions overclaimed

The draft says the exterior marks reproduce the exact number and shape of the counters inside.

The notebook authorizes a narrower statement: on some examples, exterior impressions can make information about the enclosed quantity inspectable without immediately opening the envelope.

Do not promote a bounded functional relation into a universal exact correspondence unless the source authority specifically supports it for the example being narrated.

### Failure 4 — rhetorical token→tablet genealogy recreated after being formally denied

The draft moves rhetorically through:

`counters → sealed envelope → exterior impressions → why keep counters inside? → solid numerical tablet`

It later says history was not a simple linear ladder, but the listener has already experienced a linear developmental argument.

A final caveat does not cancel the causal/genealogical structure created by the preceding narration.

## Root diagnosis

The current Writer environment successfully encourages story. The remaining failure is that the model uses **causal invention as narrative glue** when the evidence supplies coexistence, affordance, and material contrast but does not supply a documented developmental sequence.

Therefore the next change should not make the Writer more cautious in general. That would risk collapsing back into essay prose.

The required distinction is narrower:

> Narrative continuity is allowed. Unsupported historical causality is not.

The Writer may connect two practices in narration because they coexist, contrast, share a material problem, or reveal a change in affordance. The Writer may not imply that practice A historically produced practice B, that people adopted B because A failed, or that an observed affordance was the motive for invention unless that causal bridge is explicitly supported.

## Required next-round adjustment

Prepare a new clean Writer run from the current v1 experiment with the smallest possible change to Writer guidance.

Add a compact constraint equivalent to:

> You may create continuity in the telling, but do not invent a causal bridge between historical practices. A material contrast, coexistence, or change in affordance does not by itself mean one practice caused, solved, replaced, or developed into another. If the notebook does not support why a new practice appeared, narrate what changes in the material arrangement without supplying the missing historical motive.

This is a truth/authorship boundary, not a required prose line and not a new story method.

Do not add a list of banned phrases or a checklist of anti-essay techniques.

If used, this field is for pre-draft self-constraint only. It must not force the Writer to explain uncertainty in the prose.
