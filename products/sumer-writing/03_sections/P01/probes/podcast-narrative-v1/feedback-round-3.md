## Truth-precision failures to remove

### 1. Implied fraud or tampering problem

The opening describes loose counters as completely open to intervention and says that anyone could remove, divide, or regroup them. The passage then immediately introduces the clay envelope.

Even without an explicit “therefore,” this recreates the problem-to-solution grammar rejected after Round 1. The notebook does not establish that vulnerability to tampering caused envelopes to appear or was their universal purpose.

Enter through an attested material state or operation instead. Do not use an inferred administrative problem as the source of momentum.

### 2. Unsupported micro-reconstruction

The draft adds a thumb pressed into the clay, rotational shaping, a seam made to disappear, dry air, and a visible lightening of clay colour. These details are plausible, but the bounded notebook authorizes only the representative sequence of enclosing counters, closing the envelope, optionally marking or sealing its exterior, and letting it dry.

Specificity must come from attested objects, measurements, surfaces, marks, or explicitly licensed representative actions—not from an invented manufacturing technique.

### 3. Physical state stronger than the authority

The claim that the counters no longer move freely and are “locked solid” is not supplied by the notebook and may misdescribe objects enclosed in a hollow core.

The supported state change is narrower: while the envelope remains closed, the counters are not directly visible or inspectable.

### 4. Exterior marks and sealings overclaimed

“Reflecting the order” of the hidden contents is stronger than the authorized statement that exterior impressions on some examples can make information about the enclosed quantity inspectable.

Likewise, a seal impression may mark an association with authority, custody, or witnessing. It does not identify one specific act of authentication, speaker, operator, or event.

Keep “some examples” locally attached to any exterior-impression claim. Do not turn a bounded association into a unique event.

### 5. New functions introduced for dramatic continuity

The passage says the hardened marks permit observation, recording, and transfer of the object. Transfer is not authorized by the notebook. The later language of protection also converts enclosure into a universal purpose.

Do not add protection, transfer, fraud prevention, long-distance administration, or another purpose merely because it makes the process feel consequential.

### 6. Absolute and moralized ending

The ending moves from the supported fact that opening destroys the sealed state to “only one path,” “absolute verification,” “the truth inside,” and a price that must be paid. That rhetoric supplies a climax by intensifying the claim beyond the evidence.

The physical asymmetry is already strong enough: an intact envelope hides its contents; seeing the contents requires losing that intact sealed state. Let that material consequence carry the weight without turning it into a universal philosophy of truth.
