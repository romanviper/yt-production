# Narrative Identity Reset Research — toàn tác phẩm

Status: `draft_research`, 2026-09-10.  
Purpose: exploratory route selection + targeted research for the whole-work reset. This dossier supplements the existing source/claim ledgers; it does not retroactively mark old claims approved for a new architecture.

## 1. Câu hỏi được mở lại

Đề tài là lịch sử chữ viết/cuneiform, nhưng câu chuyện không được suy ra bằng cách liệt kê những năng lực của writing rồi dành cho Writer nhiệm vụ làm từng năng lực trở nên điện ảnh.

Câu hỏi exploratory mới là:

> Điều gì xảy ra với quan hệ giữa người nói/người viết và người phải hiểu lời ấy khi dấu vết có thể tồn tại lâu hơn trí nhớ, đi xa hơn giọng nói, đi qua những người không cùng ngôn ngữ, rồi cuối cùng tồn tại ngay cả khi không còn ai biết đọc?

Câu hỏi này chưa phải thesis phải chứng minh. Nó được dùng để tìm những tình huống lịch sử có diễn biến thật và để kiểm tra xem một hành trình 60–120 phút có thể tích lũy được hay không.

## 2. Ba route toàn tập đã so sánh

### Route A — “Writing mở rộng những gì con người có thể mang trên vật chất”

**Người nghe theo:** widening capacity: quantity/category → language → correspondence/law/literature → archive → survival.

**Material mạnh:** proto-cuneiform Uruk; phonetic/rebus development; genres; archives; scribal education; multilingual adaptation; decipherment.

**Ưu điểm:** chronology rõ, nguồn hiện có dày, dễ giải thích lịch sử kỹ thuật.

**Vấn đề:** phần giữa rất dễ quay lại đúng cấu trúc baseline: mỗi chapter đại diện cho một capability/function. Các case có nguy cơ trở thành ví dụ cho luận điểm “writing can X”. Việc đổi prose không chữa được tính interchangeable của các chapter.

**Quyết định:** không chọn làm spine. Capacity vẫn là exposition cần thiết nhưng không sở hữu cấu trúc.

### Route B — “Một công nghệ quyền lực: record biến con người thành thứ có thể quản lý”

**Người nghe theo:** pressure của administration, classification, audit, command, law và archive; ai có quyền ghi/đọc; khi record thắng hoặc thất bại trước con người.

**Material mạnh:** Uruk administrative corpus; institutional scale; later record/archives; Marduk-mushallim letter showing a royal order was not implemented; asymmetric scribal access.

**Ưu điểm:** human stakes và power rõ; dễ có conflict.

**Vấn đề:** research hiện tại không support một causal arc đơn giản “writing created state control”. Nếu biến mỗi case thành bằng chứng cho control, route sẽ vừa thesis-led vừa overclaim. Nó cũng làm literature, bilingual preservation và decipherment thành phụ lục khó tích lũy tự nhiên.

**Quyết định:** không chọn làm toàn-spine. Giữ power/action như một stress test quan trọng ở giữa.

### Route C — “Một lời nhắn tìm người đọc”

**Người nghe theo:** khoảng cách ngày càng lớn giữa người tạo dấu và người phải làm dấu ấy có nghĩa. Mỗi chặng thay đổi điều kiện của cuộc gặp giữa text và reader: trí nhớ của messenger; reader vắng mặt; người học phải được tạo ra; reader nói ngôn ngữ khác; community chỉ còn các chuyên gia; cuối cùng không còn reader; rồi một community mới dựng lại khả năng đọc.

**Material ban đầu có thể gánh:**

- Enmerkar literary tradition: message vượt sức oral repetition và được đặt lên clay.
- Uruk IV–III tablets: archaeology sớm hơn chủ yếu administrative, language thường undetermined; continuous voice không dễ recover.
- Phonetic/logosyllabic development: signs dần có thể encode language rõ hơn.
- Old Babylonian letters: portable clay carries information across time/space; Marduk-mushallim reports that a king's orders are not being carried out.
- Old Babylonian schooling: thousands of school texts at Nippur and other sites; small-scale training reproduces scribal competence.
- Sumerian-Akkadian bilingual lexical tradition: Sumerian persists as learned/cultic language after vernacular decline; readers are deliberately taught access to a language no longer native to them.
- Seleucid/Parthian bilingual learned tablets: Sumerian with Akkadian translations survives among specialists even after Aramaic/Greek dominate speech.
- Latest-known cuneiform anchors in the first century CE; living competence subsequently disappears, but objects remain.
- Nineteenth-century decipherment: multiple scholars rebuild readings through copied inscriptions, comparison, hypothesis and testing; the 1857 Royal Asiatic Society translation comparison provides a concrete verification episode.

**Điểm khác baseline:** chapter không tồn tại vì “writing acquires function X”. Chúng tồn tại vì quan hệ text–reader gặp một điều kiện mới. Mỗi movement làm khoảng cách khó hơn: memory → absence → institutional action → training → language change → specialist survival → competence break → recovered reading.

**Rủi ro:** route dễ rơi vào khẩu hiệu “writing needs readers” lặp lại tám lần. Để tránh điều đó, mỗi movement chỉ được giữ nếu có một historical development khác thật sự và consequence riêng; “reader” là quan hệ xuyên suốt, không phải kết luận duy nhất.

**Quyết định:** **chọn Route C**. Nó giữ writing làm đối tượng xuyên suốt nhưng đặt trọng lượng vào con người, có opening/middle/ending nối nhau, và cho phép material kỹ thuật xuất hiện đúng lúc người nghe cần hiểu nó.

## 3. Research theo route đã chọn

### 3.1. Literary opening: một message vượt khỏi messenger

Trong *Enmerkar and the Lord of Aratta*, lines 500–514, speech của Enmerkar trở nên substantial/extensive; messenger không thể repeat; lord of Kulaba pats clay và writes the message as on a tablet. Lines 518–535 tiếp tục với việc clay tablet được mang tới lord of Aratta và người nhận được yêu cầu examine/learn its content.

**Status:** documented Sumerian literary tradition, không phải eyewitness report về invention. Existing P01 research notes surviving witnesses are Old Babylonian and therefore much later than Uruk IV proto-cuneiform.

**Story value:** đây là ancient imagination of the text–reader relation: lời nói không còn phải sống hoàn toàn trong memory của messenger. Nó tạo một promise rất rõ rồi cho archaeology phá vỡ sự đơn giản của promise đó.

**Sources / locators:**  
- NIR-SRC-001 — ETCSL, *Enmerkar and the Lord of Aratta*, lines 500–535: https://etcsl.orinst.ox.ac.uk/section1/tr1823.htm  
- Reuse verified scope from `P01-narrative-research.md`, including CDLI composite/witness dating and the prohibition against treating this as an historical invention event.

### 3.2. Archaeological reversal: tablet sớm tồn tại, nhưng voice chưa hiện ra rõ

CDLI object records from Uruk IV/III repeatedly classify tablets as **Administrative** and language as **Undetermined**. Examples include P001138 (Uruk IV, ca. 3350–3200 BCE), P001235 (Uruk IV) and P000734 (Uruk III, ca. 3200–3000 BCE). These are object anchors, not statistical proof for the whole corpus. Existing global synthesis supports the broader conclusion that the earliest substantial corpus has an administrative center of gravity and that continuous speech/underlying language are often not securely recoverable.

**Historical development:** the earliest surviving written world does not arrive as a letter waiting for an absent reader. Much of what can be reconstructed first is quantity/category/structured relation. Writing is already useful before modern scholars can confidently hear continuous language in it.

**Story consequence:** Enmerkar's perfect image becomes a mystery rather than an origin explanation. The audience now needs to know what has to change for marks to become capable of carrying a message as language.

**Sources / locators:**  
- NIR-SRC-002 — CDLI P001138, Uruk IV administrative tablet, language undetermined: https://cdli.earth/P001138  
- NIR-SRC-003 — CDLI P001235, Uruk IV administrative tablet, language undetermined: https://cdli.earth/P001235  
- NIR-SRC-004 — CDLI P000734, Uruk III administrative tablet, language undetermined: https://cdli.earth/P000734  
- NIR-SRC-005 — CDLI, Englund 1994 *Archaic Administrative Texts from Uruk*: https://www.cdli.earth/publications/1139  
- Existing authority: `research-synthesis.md`, sections 1–3.

**Boundary:** administrative predominance in surviving material does not prove administration was the only use/cause. “Undetermined language” does not mean users lacked spoken language or that no early sign had phonetic value.

### 3.3. From relation to language: the absent reader becomes possible

Existing synthesis establishes the bounded technical development: rare phonetic/rebus uses appear early, while consistent phonetic writing becomes clearer in the third millennium BCE; cuneiform becomes logo-syllabic. The exact threshold is contested and should not be dramatized as a single invention day.

**Why this exposition belongs here:** without some phonological/language encoding, the later story about a reader recovering the writer's words cannot happen. This is not a chapter about a feature of writing for its own sake; it solves the problem created by the opening.

**Boundary:** do not label the entire earliest corpus securely Sumerian; do not imply a natural evolutionary ladder toward alphabetic writing.

**Source base:** reuse existing reviewed claim/source graph in `claim-ledger.json`, `source-index.json` and `research-synthesis.md` for early phonetic/rebus and language-attribution claims. No new causal claim is added here.

### 3.4. A message can arrive; reality may refuse it

A Metropolitan Museum Old Babylonian private letter (86.11.111, ca. 1632 BCE) is attributed to Marduk-mushallim. The museum describes him reporting to a superior about implementation of a king's order, probably Ammisaduqa. The king wants security increased around Sippar-Yahrurum; Marduk-mushallim warns that orders, including closing the city gate at night, are not being implemented.

**Historical development:** now the medium can carry an articulated instruction/report across distance. The interesting event is not simply “writing can command”. The text survives because command and action have separated: the reader can know what authority wanted precisely because people did not necessarily do it.

**Story consequence:** this prevents the route from turning into technological triumphalism. A text can bridge absence, but a reader/recipient/institution must still interpret and act. Persistence of words is not persistence of obedience.

**Source / locator:**  
- NIR-SRC-006 — The Metropolitan Museum of Art, object 86.11.111, *Cuneiform tablet: private letter*, ca. 1632 BCE: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/321706

**Boundary:** one unusual letter is not representative proof that royal orders usually failed, and it cannot establish the cause of the later collapse of Babylonian state. Use it as a documented situation testing the relation between written authority and action.

### 3.5. To preserve writing, a society must manufacture new readers

ORACC's Old Babylonian School overview describes abundant evidence for scribal training ca. 2000–1595 BCE. Archaeological finds at Nippur, Ur, Isin, Sippar and Kish include thousands of school texts; at Nippur many came from private domestic buildings interpreted as schools. ORACC characterizes Old Babylonian education as small-scale/private and transmitted from one generation to the next, serving both professional training and transmission of Mesopotamian cultural heritage.

The Met student exercise tablet (ca. 20th–16th century BCE) gives the process physical scale: a small lenticular tablet where a teacher writes a form and a student repeats it on the reverse; the example has the divine name Urash copied repeatedly.

**Historical development:** the problem of the reader changes. The writer can no longer assume a recipient already exists; institutions/households must repeatedly produce people capable of turning wedges into language. Copying is not merely school texture—it is how the chain of possible future readers is rebuilt.

**Sources / locators:**  
- NIR-SRC-007 — ORACC, *The Old Babylonian School*: https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/obmc/OldBabylonianSchool/index.html  
- NIR-SRC-008 — The Met, student exercise tablet, object 321878: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/321878

**Boundary:** do not project Old Babylonian school organization back to Uruk; do not infer population literacy rates from school-tablet survival.

### 3.6. The reader can be born into another language

ORACC DCCLT summarizes a long bilingual lexical tradition. Akkadian and Sumerian were used side by side in the third millennium; by the end of the third millennium Sumerian ceased to be a spoken language but remained in scribal/cultic use. Sumerian lexical lists received Akkadian translations to teach and preserve access to the no-longer-vernacular language. Old Babylonian sign/word lists formed structured curricula; many continued in scribal education into the first millennium BCE.

A concrete Old Babylonian Nippur bilingual lexical tablet, CDLI P228603, preserves Sumerian forms paired with Akkadian equivalents. Another CDLI feature notes Old Babylonian school curriculum centered heavily on Sumerian although Akkadian was vernacular.

**Historical development:** writing is no longer merely crossing physical absence. It crosses a linguistic break. A learner can become the reader of words from a language that is not the language of ordinary speech around them.

**Story consequence:** the text–reader relation becomes an act of deliberate cultural maintenance. This gives the later survival of Sumerian literature a mechanism, not just a claim that clay is durable.

**Sources / locators:**  
- NIR-SRC-009 — ORACC DCCLT, lexical tradition introduction: https://oracc.museum.upenn.edu/dcclt/intro/lexical_intro.html  
- NIR-SRC-010 — CDLI P228603, Old Babylonian bilingual lexical/school tablet from Nippur: https://cdli.earth/P228603  
- NIR-SRC-011 — CDLI Tablet feature on Old Babylonian Sumerian curriculum: https://cdli.earth/cdli-tablet/188

**Boundary:** vernacular decline is regional/gradual, not a single death date. Script survival, language survival and institutional survival must be kept separate.

### 3.7. Centuries later, specialist readers keep dead languages audible

A Met tablet dated roughly 2nd–1st century BCE records a balag lament. The museum notes a Seleucid-period pattern: words in Sumerian with many lines accompanied by Akkadian translation. It also notes that by then Sumerian had long ceased ordinary speech and Akkadian probably was no longer widely spoken either, while Aramaic and Greek dominated much of the region; learned scribes nevertheless continued writing both Sumerian and Akkadian in cuneiform into the early centuries CE.

**Historical development:** the reader has become a specialist sustaining access to layered dead/learned languages. The tablet carries a lament tradition over immense time not because material alone guarantees memory, but because successive scribes keep reconstructing the relation between marks, pronunciation and meaning.

**Source / locator:**  
- NIR-SRC-012 — The Met, *a-she-er gi-ta, balag to Innin/Ishtar*, object 30000538 / related catalogue entry: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/30000538

**Boundary:** a surviving late copy demonstrates late learned transmission; it is not transparent evidence for the exact wording/performance of every earlier stage of the tradition.

### 3.8. The chain contracts and breaks; the clay does not tell us the exact last reader

The Met's Late Babylonian grammatical-text catalogue states that the last dated cuneiform text has a date corresponding to 75 CE, while allowing that the script probably continued afterward. Encyclopaedia Iranica likewise places the latest known cuneiform texts in the first century CE before the script sank into oblivion.

**Historical development:** for the first time in the route, there is no longer a reliable living chain from marks to readers. The material remains, but competence is no longer continuously transmitted into the later world that rediscovers it.

**Sources / locators:**  
- NIR-SRC-013 — The Met, *Late Babylonian grammatical text*, object 321677: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/321677  
- NIR-SRC-014 — Encyclopaedia Iranica, *Cuneiform Script*: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cuneiform-script/

**Boundary:** 75 CE is a latest-known dated anchor in current evidence, not “the day cuneiform died” and not proof of an identifiable last reader.

### 3.9. A new community of readers is built after the break

Iranica's history of decipherment shows a distributed nineteenth-century process rather than one heroic revelation. Grotefend used recurring royal-name/genealogy patterns to establish approximate Old Persian sign values; Rask, Burnouf, Lassen and others added grammatical/phonetic constraints; Rawlinson copied and published the Bisotun material and worked across versions; Edward Hincks independently solved key properties of Babylonian cuneiform. Disagreements remained substantial.

In 1857, W. H. Fox Talbot proposed a concrete test: several scholars independently translated an Assyrian inscription of Tiglath-Pileser I. Rawlinson, Hincks and others submitted versions; a Royal Asiatic Society jury compared them and judged the substantial congruence a success for the decipherment.

**Historical development:** after continuity is broken, reading returns not by inheriting a teacher's practice but by rebuilding constraints from names, languages, parallel inscriptions, sign behavior and independent tests. The final reader is therefore not one genius but a new scholarly community constructing a competence no living Mesopotamian school could hand to them.

**Story consequence / ending:** the opening question reverses. Enmerkar imagined writing freeing a message from one messenger's memory. The end shows the limit of that freedom: clay can outlive every intended reader, yet words remain inaccessible until humans manufacture a new relation to the signs.

**Sources / locators:**  
- NIR-SRC-015 — Encyclopaedia Iranica, *Rawlinson, Henry ii. Contributions to Assyriology and Iranian Studies*: https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/rawlinson-ii/  
- NIR-SRC-014 — Iranica, *Cuneiform Script* (Grotefend/Rask/Lassen/Rawlinson chronology and method): https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/cuneiform-script/

**Boundary:** do not collapse decipherment into Rawlinson alone; do not describe modern decipherment as unbroken transmission from ancient scribes.

## 4. Chuyển động toàn tập được research support

Research hiện support một journey gồm bảy narrative movements (production sections có thể chia khác):

1. **The promise and the reversal** — Enmerkar imagines words escaping messenger memory; earlier Uruk evidence gives mostly administrative, language-undetermined tablets.
2. **Making an absent reader possible** — language-bearing cuneiform becomes increasingly explicit; the audience earns the technical explanation because it solves the opening problem.
3. **When the message reaches power but not reality** — Marduk-mushallim gives a concrete test of written instruction/report versus implementation.
4. **Manufacturing readers** — Old Babylonian education turns reading/writing into competence deliberately reproduced across generations.
5. **Reading across language death/change** — bilingual curricula and learned Sumerian let readers access a language no longer vernacular.
6. **The narrowing chain** — late specialist scribes sustain Sumerian/Akkadian cuneiform traditions while wider linguistic worlds change; eventually current evidence reaches a competence break without an identifiable last reader.
7. **Readers return** — nineteenth-century decipherment reconstructs reading by collective hypothesis, comparison and testing.

The movement is cumulative: every later stage increases the distance a text must cross, and therefore changes what a “reader” has to be. Removing schooling would make bilingual/dead-language survival mechanistically unexplained; removing bilingual learned tradition would make the late competence break merely a date; removing the break would make decipherment a curiosity rather than the reversal that completes the opening.

## 5. What is deliberately not selected

- No universal history of all writing systems.
- No deterministic token → tablet → alphabet ladder.
- No eight-function checklist carried forward just because old research exists.
- No thesis that writing autonomously causes state, obedience, memory or civilization.
- No invented “last scribe” death scene.
- No claim that Enmerkar preserves the actual invention of writing.
- No claim that Sumerian/cuneiform is direct ancestor of all modern writing.

## 6. Research limitations remaining

- Early phonetic/rebus transition is intentionally reused from the existing reviewed evidence graph rather than re-researched from scratch in this pass; full prose should resolve exact exemplars/locators before expansion.
- The selected Marduk-mushallim letter is a strong bounded situation, not a representative sample of Old Babylonian command compliance.
- The late first-century-CE endpoint remains a latest-known evidence anchor, not a biographical “last reader”.
- The route focuses on Mesopotamian/cuneiform tradition and selected later Persian/Assyriological decipherment evidence. It does not claim to tell the universal history of human writing.

These limits do not block an outline/compression because the route's major transformations have source-supported material. They must remain visible if later full prose requests narrower causal or chronological claims.
